Session Fourteen — Simple Plan
Stage One — Morning After the Ruins
The party wakes west of Jelbin after repairing the ancient support structure and killing the Owlbear. The supernatural dread is gone, the magical pulse is stable, and the area feels noticeably more normal than before. This should strongly reinforce the idea that repairing the structure worked.
Stage Two — Return to Jelbin
When they return, the livestock are already beginning to recover. Animals are no longer pressing desperately against eastern fences, some are willing to leave their enclosures again, and Farmer Ryl can visibly confirm that the problem is easing. Elder Farin considers the Guild quest successfully completed.
Stage Three — Explain What Happened
The locals naturally ask what caused the problem, giving the party a chance to decide how much they want to reveal about the ancient magical structure. They can describe the Owlbear, the ruins, both, or keep the stranger details private. If they documented the strange symbol or structure, this is a good time to make sure they still have that information.
Stage Four — Travel Toward Hollowmere
With Jelbin resolved, the party continues along their planned route toward Hollowmere rather than immediately returning to Stonebridge. Travel can be mostly uneventful, with an optional traveler or merchant mentioning that bear activity around Hollowmere has become increasingly dangerous. This section can mostly be left open for normal party interaction and whatever nonsense naturally occurs.
Stage Five — Arrival at Hollowmere
Hollowmere should immediately feel more tense than Jelbin. Livestock are being brought inside early, damaged fences are common, hunters move in groups, and locals are nervous because bears have started approaching inhabited areas far more frequently than normal. The village believes it has a bear problem, but experienced hunters insist the behavior is unusual.
Stage Six — Initial Investigation
The party can speak with a village authority, a local hunter, and someone directly affected by an attack. They learn that the bears seem exhausted, hungry, frightened, or unusually aggressive, and that they are all coming from the direction of the Elderward. The key conclusion should be that the bears are not simply expanding their territory—something appears to be pushing them out of it.
Optional Stage Seven — Bear Encounter
If the party immediately decides to hunt one down, have a single bear encounter prepared. The bear should behave more like a displaced, frightened animal than a normal predator, and Speak with Animals can reveal simple ideas such as “home bad,” “trees wrong,” or “had to leave.” Whether they kill it, calm it, or track it, the evidence should point back toward the Elderward.
Session Goal
By the end of Session Fourteen, the Jelbin problem should feel definitively solved, reinforcing the party’s belief that repairing ancient structures is helpful. Hollowmere should then establish the next escalation: animals are no longer merely sensing danger—they are actively abandoning the Elderward because of it.
