# Session Twelve
**Jelbin Crossroads - Something is Wrong**

Whatever they desire to do in stonebridge. They start in the office, and Marrek tells them they better get on with their mission, Copper Ranks.

## Stage One - Arrival at Jelbin

The party arrives at Jelbin Crossroads. The settlement appears normal at first glance, no different than the last time the party traveled through here. People walk the streets, travelers stop at the tavern, and shops are open.

Speaking to a Village Elder, they bring a few people into the conversation. Elder Farin (female), Farmer Ryl (male), Tavernkeep (If Shadow remembers the name I gave, otherwise it will be Carn (male))

Other farmers if need: Farmer Pax (male), Farmer Kelia (female), Farmer Quill (female)

Livestock are often refusing pasture. Western farms, like the one owned by Ryl, report their livestock refuse to come out for pasture at all. Travel animals have become harder to manage, and several farmers are beginning to worry about shortages and loss of income. They filed with the Guild because the locals haven't found an ordinary explanation.

The Tavernkeep provides rumors and patterns. Travelers coming from the east and south report no animal issues until approaching Jelbin. A few travelers coming from the west have complained about uneasy or nervous horses, but experienced riders have gotten through without incident. The tavernkeep may also mention beyond the first talk that there have been rumors of a large creature out to the west. Trees have been found clawed up and a small number of livestock have disapeared.

Farmers provide evidence. Their animals are healthy. Their food and water are normal. There are no obvious signs of disease, but animals that would normally happily wander outside have increasingly refused or stayed close to their barns, fenced yards, or enclosed spaces.

## Investigation

Speak with Animals:
Generally the animals will say that the outside is bad, freightening, or wrong. More details can be derived from smarter details, like dogs or horses. They might inculde the direction of west. The animals will include that travelling is unsafe, and smarter ones agree that the west is unsafe.

**Trained versus untrained animals**
Livestock, like cows, sheep, chicken, or farm dogs respond more to instinct than anything else. Getting one to move west voluntarily becomes difficult, very difficult once they are certain distance from home. To the east or south, animals will move calmly or quickly with a bit of couersion to get them out of their enclosure. A good riding horse can easily continue, but farm animals will not. As they travel farther west, even trained horses begin requiring assurance and competent handling. At a certain point, they will go no further.

**Owlbear as backup**
As the players investigate, they may find signs of the Owlbear.

Clues:
Damaged Fencing (bite or claw marks)
Large Claw Marks (trees/rocks)
feathers caught on brush
oversized tracks
scratched trees
partially eaten carcass
disturbed ground suggesting something large moved through the area

The tracks lead west. None of the other farms will have these clues.

Animals refuse to go west, and a large predator appears to have fled this way from the west. An NPC can say, "Odd thing is, whatever it is seems to be coming towards us. Animals usually avoid settlements, even predators, yet it is coming close enough for us to notice."

## Leaving the Road
The support structure is sufficently far from the main road that ordinary travelers wouldn't stumble into it. A mile or two traveling west from the village will do. Any animals with them, aproximately a thousand feet away will refuse to go further. They pass through scrubland before eventually they'll see something past the hills in the distance. Too small to make out the distance.

Once the party gets within 500 feet, they will begin to feel it themselves. They will all roll wisdom saving throws. Low Rolls (1-10) will result in a noticable rise in a feeling of dread, while 11-15 impose a small feeling of something not being right, and 16-20 means the person remains uneffected, but still can feel tension in the air. The Spellcasters will notice a rise in background magical energy slowly building before a pulse bursts out, this stabilizes into a pulse that releases every 30 seconds. The closer they get the stronger it feels.

**Owlbear**

About three hundred feet away from the party. They'll have been signs of it being here beforehand. Clawed trees and rocks. Feathers left around. It will have seemed to have been drawn here, but also unwilling to get closer to it.

The Owlbear will not immediately engage, and will not remove closer to the ruin. It growls, backs away slightly to the side, seem to have been searching for another escape. Attacks if the party either continues to approach or deliberates for too long. They should get that it seems not to want to approach them, nor go towards the structure. If Speak with Animals is cast, its understanding is primative: "Bad Stone" "Need to be here" "Hurts" "Away" "Want away. Not far". If they approach it in battle, it will attack furciously, but not try to kill the party. If an openning to flee east appears, it will disengage and flee.

## The Secondary Support Structure

The structure is ancient and yet sophisciated. Intricate patterns are upon every stone of the structure, which about 30 feet across, in a hexagonal/circle pattern. There appears to be one primary damaged stone in the center, the magical pulses originate here. Six points surround it with stone patterns in the ground hard to see through the grass that has grown in. These points are not all intact, two are intact, three are gone, and the last one appears to have been clawed till it tipped over and fell, however appears that with a bit of strength, it could be placed upright.

Detect Magic reveals: Abjuration Magic. Not exclusively however. There is a faint magical signature the spellcaster fails to interpret. Perhaps conjuration or something adjacent to it. The structure isn't merely blocking magic, it is seemingly interacting with something elsewhere. A successful arcana check will establish that it appears to be regulating or containing magical pressure. A very good result gives: the magic does not appear to orginate here.

**Party Decision**

They may leave it untouched. They can document the structure, symbols, and bring the information back. If they ask about symbols, I will say "They appear to be lines moving in intricate patterns across the stones" (OOC: like electronic power lines on a motherboard). The thing will continue Pulsing regardless.

Repair it:

They can restore the displaced stones, attempting to place the stone knocked over by the Owlbear upwards or otherwise attempt a repair that makes sense, it will succeed. The Pulses stabalize, the oppressive feeling diminishes severely. If they do this, it will succeed, no rolls needed. Animals around Jelbin will slowly return to normal, causing a completed quest.

Damage or destroy it:

The structure, if broken at the main pillar or secondary pillars, will release a powerful magic wave the entire party can detect, as it will also launch them back, knocking them prone. The spellcasters will feel the presence completely gone. Detect magic will reveal only trace elements. This will cause the local effect to end because the support transmission isn't regulating or transmitting. The village will improve, though a large uproar will happen when the animals attempt to indiscriminately flee for a few minutes of pure fear. Things will slowly return to normal, slower than repairing it. The Guild will consider this a success
