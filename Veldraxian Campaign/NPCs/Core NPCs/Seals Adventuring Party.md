I’d establish a core five-person Seals Adventuring Party, with the Grandfather, Marrek, the Cleric, and the Rogue as the original members, and the Fifth Member joining during the later stages. The Grandfather’s wider career could involve many other companions, but these five are the ones remembered as the party connected to the seals.

The Grandfather — Arcane Scholar

Already established: an elderly half-elf wizard whose adventuring career involved extensive travel, scholarship, and extraplanar disturbances. He became the party’s primary theorist, interpreting the ancient structures and developing the methods used to reinforce them.

He was not necessarily the formal leader, but once the seals became the party’s focus, his knowledge increasingly determined their direction.

Marrek — Human Fighter

Suggested class: Fighter, likely Battle Master
Race: Human
Current age: Late fifties or early sixties
Party role: Front-line defender and field commander

Marrek was a heavily armored sword-and-shield fighter, accustomed to holding narrow passages and protecting less durable companions while they completed more specialized work. He was not an academic and understood little of the deeper extraplanar theory, but he trusted the Grandfather’s expertise because reinforcing the seals appeared to stop the immediate disasters.

He was the person who made decisions once combat began. The Grandfather might determine what needed to be done, but Marrek decided how the party survived long enough to do it.

Before joining the party, Marrek could have been a professional caravan guard or junior military officer who became frustrated with rigid command structures. Adventuring gave him greater freedom to protect people directly.

After the party fractured, he concluded that clearer preparation, better assignments, and stronger oversight could prevent similar deaths. That eventually led him to become Stonebridge’s Guildmaster, where he could control which missions were posted and which parties were trusted with them. He knows the Seals Party’s battles and personal history, but not the full magical investigation—matching his current admission that he worked with the Grandfather without knowing everything.

Suggested full name: Marrek Dain
Simple, practical, and consistent with the existing “Marrek.”

The Cleric — Dwarven Healer

Suggested class: Cleric, Life Domain
Race: Hill dwarf
Current age: Approximately 150
Party role: Healer, diplomat, chronicler, and relief worker

A dwarf works particularly well because she can still be alive without requiring extraordinary magic, while age and exhaustion can convincingly explain why she no longer adventures.

She did not join because she was interested in ancient ruins. She first encountered the others while treating survivors of the unrelated disaster that initially brought them together. When the disturbances continued, she stayed because the victims needed someone who could provide healing, reassurance, and practical assistance.

While the Grandfather and Rogue investigated the causes, she handled the effects:

treating magical illnesses,
caring for displaced families,
calming frightened communities,
recording symptoms and animal behavior,
and negotiating access to settlements suspicious of adventurers.

She supported continued intervention because she personally saw what happened when the party delayed. Even if the Rogue’s doubts concerned her, the suffering in front of her was immediate.

The Fifth Member was eventually assigned to protect her during relief work. Their relationship became deeply emotional but remained unnamed. They always assumed there would be time to define it after the crisis.

There was not.

During the final disaster, she was forced to choose between saving the Grandfather, Marrek, and the Rogue together or attempting to reach the isolated Fifth Member. She chose the three.

She has never forgiven herself.

Today she maintains a small rural chapel, perhaps in Harth or near the edge of Highreach. She still heals anyone who comes through her door, but no longer possesses the emotional strength to place herself at the center of another great crisis.

Suggested name: Sister Brenna Hearthmantle

“Brenna” feels warm and grounded, while “Hearthmantle” reflects her role as someone who protects ordinary people rather than seeking glory.

The Rogue — Elven Scout

Suggested class: Rogue, Scout
Race: Wood elf
Current age: Approximately 190
Party role: Scout, infiltrator, investigator, and field alchemist

A wood elf gives him the longevity necessary to remain relevant while naturally supporting his stealth, tracking, and wilderness expertise. It also distinguishes him from Marlo: this Rogue is not primarily an urban criminal, but someone who can move through ruins, forests, caves, and hostile territory without being noticed.

Before the party formed, he may have worked as a smuggler, independent guide, relic thief, or finder of missing people. He understood illegal networks and deception, but was never fundamentally cruel.

His partnership with the Grandfather became essential:

The Rogue found concealed entrances and buried mechanisms.
The Grandfather interpreted what he recovered.
The Rogue observed how animals and monsters reacted.
The Grandfather transformed those observations into theory.

The Grandfather explained more to him than to Marrek or the Cleric because the Rogue needed to know what evidence to seek. As a result, he became the only member capable of seriously challenging the Grandfather’s conclusions.

He noticed that every successful reinforcement ended one disturbance but seemed to correspond with another beginning elsewhere. He saw mechanisms react in ways the Grandfather dismissed and became convinced that the party was not repairing simple barriers.

After the Fifth Member died, the Rogue left as soon as the Cleric had healed him enough to travel.

He did not become a murderer or gang leader. Instead, he disappeared into an illegal but not inherently malicious life. My preferred version is that he became a hidden apothecary and information broker operating under an assumed identity. He produces antidotes, field medicines, smoke compounds, sleeping draughts, poisons, and other goods unavailable through legal channels. He occasionally helps fugitives, smugglers, adventurers, and poor communities that cannot obtain official assistance.

He is morally gray, deeply cynical, and extraordinarily difficult to find—but not evil.

The Cleric encountered him once after the fracture. He returned something belonging to the Fifth Member, spoke briefly, and disappeared again.

Suggested name: Caelren Vey

The name is light enough to sound elven, while “Vey” is short and easily replaced by aliases during his hidden life.

The Fifth Member — Human Temple Warden

Suggested class: Paladin, likely Oath of the Watchers or Devotion
Race: Human
Age at death: Late twenties or early thirties
Party role: Cleric’s protector and specialist against unnatural creatures

A paladin gives him a distinct function without making him central to the seal theory.

Marrek was the party’s overall tank and battlefield commander. The Fifth Member was a dedicated protector, assigned specifically to accompany the Cleric into dangerous settlements and guard her while she treated victims. His divine senses and resistance to supernatural influence also made him useful against extraplanar creatures, curses, and unstable magical environments.

He joined after the seal investigation was already well underway. A religious order sent him to protect the Cleric after several relief missions became increasingly dangerous.

Initially, he was an escort.

Then he stayed.

His role became indispensable because the Cleric frequently had to separate from the others to tend victims, negotiate with communities, or establish safe areas. He protected her while the core party investigated the sites themselves.

He also became the party’s emotional bridge.

He trained with Marrek, assisted the Rogue during dangerous scouting operations, listened attentively to the Grandfather’s explanations, and worked beside the Cleric. Because he arrived after the original relationships and arguments had formed, he was less entrenched in their disagreements.

His bond with the Cleric was never formally declared romantic, but everyone understood it was becoming something more.

During the final seal disaster, he was isolated on the far side of the destabilization—possibly protecting civilians, holding back a creature, or attempting to secure an escape route. The Cleric could either attempt to reach him or save the other three surviving members.

He understood her choice.

His final act should make that clear. He does not die believing she abandoned him. He knowingly gives her permission to save the others.

That makes her guilt more painful, not less.

Suggested name: Darian Holt

“Darian” sounds young and earnest, while “Holt” is ordinary enough that he feels like a person rather than a destined legendary hero.

Proposed Party Lineup

The Grandfather — Half-Elf Wizard; arcane theory and seal reinforcement
Marrek Dain — Human Fighter; tank and field commander
Sister Brenna Hearthmantle — Dwarf Cleric; healing, relief, diplomacy, and records
Caelren Vey — Wood-Elf Rogue; scouting, infiltration, evidence gathering, and field alchemy
Darian Holt — Human Paladin; late-joining protector assigned to Brenna

This gives each person distinct knowledge:

The Grandfather knows what he believed the seals were.
Caelren knows what the evidence actually showed.
Marrek knows how the final mission fell apart.
Brenna knows the human cost and made the decisive choice.
Darian knew enough to help, but died before the truth could be understood.

The Fifth Member’s role also works because he had a clear reason to remain without needing full access to the investigation. He was not there to understand the seals. He was there to protect the person keeping everyone alive.
